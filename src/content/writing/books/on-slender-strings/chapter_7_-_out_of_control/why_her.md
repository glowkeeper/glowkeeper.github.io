# Why Her?

My heart sank when I saw my daughter’s surgeon’s number come up on my mobile one evening in late July 2014. She had just had another scan, and I knew he wouldn’t phone unless it was important. He told me that the multidisciplinary team had seen enhancement at the tumour site and believed there had been a small recurrence. He tried to reassure me that this could settle without causing further trouble and said that the team would scan Abi again in three months and monitor her carefully.

I reeled. We had had 18 months of Abi being tumour free and I hoped that we had reached the end of her story with cancer. But it was not to be.

I phoned Sarah, conveying no emotion. I heard a small sob at the other end of the line. But it was not for me to console her; we were long past supporting each other. I’m not certain we ever did.

Unfortunately, my mother had been struggling ever since my gran had passed away a year ago. So I did not know if I could pass on the news to her. I had pondered the problem for a day or so when she called, out of the blue: “Hello Andrew. I’m afraid I have some bad news. Your cousin Peter has terminal cancer. The doctors have given him about three months to live,” she told me. I couldn’t believe it. He was my age, too! We had not been particularly close, but the bastard had managed to bowl me out in a youth cricket final, so we did share some stories. What the bloody hell was going on? First Abi, now Peter! There was no way I could burden mum with more bad news, so I asked her to pass on my love to Peter and his family and then put the phone down without letting her know about Abi.

Later on, my girlfriend called, which meant I was finally able to unburden myself. She came over to my home immediately and helped by listening. We played happy families over the next few days by taking the girls to the beach and going fruit picking. By the time she left, some normality had returned, and my mood had improved. I felt that I would be able to cope.

Even so, I couldn’t help watching Abi and asking: “why her?”

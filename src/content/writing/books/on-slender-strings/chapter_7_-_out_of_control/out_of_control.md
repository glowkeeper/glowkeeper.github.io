# Out of Control

Things appear to have escalated out of control. It is now the morning of Saturday, 20 September, and I am sat next to Abi’s bed on the medical ward of our local hospital. I rushed her into A\&E because she couldn’t stop vomiting and she’s been here for a couple of weeks. Thankfully the doctors here believe that this episode is entirely unrelated to her tumour. However, that seems scant consolation because they are still unsure as to what the problem might be and my daughter’s weight is down to just 16.1 kg, 4 kg less than it was a year ago.

To make matters worse, relations between Sarah and me, which were already dreadful, had deteriorated terribly. It all reached a new low a few days earlier, just a few hours after my daughter had undergone a relatively minor procedure to have a peripherally inserted central catheter (PICC) placed in her upper chest. That was so she could be given nutritional supplements intravenously. Both Sarah and I wanted to remain with Abi after the procedure, while Kara needed taking home for school. Because only one parent could stay overnight, the practical disagreement quickly became personal. We argued in front of the girls until Abi, distressingly, found herself trying to settle it for us. We eventually followed our earlier arrangement: I stayed with Abi and Sarah took Kara home.

Once things had calmed down, I put on a film, which Abi and I watched snuggled up on her bed. With my daughter happily asleep afterwards, I had time to reflect on all the events that had led Sarah and me to that boiling point—and on the fact that Abi should never have been placed in the middle of it.

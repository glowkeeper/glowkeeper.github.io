# Tantrum

At Friday lunchtime, I arrived at Abi’s school to take her up to the hospital for her chemotherapy. The hospital wasn’t far and it was a beautiful day, so I had decided we would walk up. I fetched Abi from her classroom and before leaving, we went to the toilet so I could put numbing cream on her port.

Then we began to make our way up the hill towards the hospital. Abi had expected us to drive and did not want to walk. I insisted. What should have been a minor disagreement quickly became something much larger.

The argument continued until Abi sat down and refused to move. I was becoming angry too, which only made things worse. Eventually I picked her up and carried her, while she struggled and shouted for someone to call the police. A passer-by stopped to check that she was safe. I explained that she was my daughter and that we were on our way to her hospital appointment, although I doubt the scene looked anything like as straightforward as I wanted it to be.

We finally made it to the hospital, where I sat Abi on a bench and tried to calm her. I told her that, whatever she might feel about me at that moment, I loved her very much. One of her nurses appeared and, with considerably more success than I had managed, helped her settle. She took Abi up to the ward while I went to the canteen and gave us both some space.

Abi was playing happily when I finally made it to the ward. It appeared I was out of the dog house. After chemotherapy, we talked about what had happened and she apologised. I accepted, though I might also have owed her an apology for allowing a disagreement about a car journey to become a battle of wills. Then she skipped down the corridor and out of the ward. It was as if the whole tantrum had never happened.

# Chemotherapy

Sarah and I met Abi’s oncologist on Monday 19 January 2015. We discussed her recent scans, the recommendations of the second opinions I had received, and my concerns regarding the efficacy and toxicity of various chemotherapy agents. Taking all that into account, he had organised an MRI of her spine, an eye test, and also an endocrine test to check her hormone levels. He recommended the chemotherapy drug vinblastine, which he expected to be relatively well tolerated while helping to control her tumours. He then arranged another meeting for 26 January, to give us some time to think things through.

Over the next few days, while Sarah took Abi to the tests and scans that her oncologist had booked, I recounted Abi’s story in an online low-grade glioma support group and asked about families’ experiences of vinblastine. Several parents described it as comparatively tolerable: their children had continued attending school and had experienced tumour stability or shrinkage, although some reported tiredness, leg pain or low blood counts. One parent said that the tumour had remained stable but her child’s eyesight had deteriorated, prompting a change of treatment. These were personal accounts, not clinical evidence, but they helped me understand what treatment might look like in everyday life.

Following this (mostly) positive feedback, I decided that I would agree to vinblastine, despite my previous concerns about chemotherapy. Besides, I wasn’t sure I had any choice now, especially given the proximity of the second tumour site to Abi’s optic nerve and the danger that it could affect her eyesight if we didn’t act.

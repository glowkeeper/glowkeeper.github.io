# Brain Tumour Statistics: Then and Now

## The Figures Available When I Wrote the Book

The original version of this page used Cancer Research UK figures from 2009 and 2010. It reported 4,987 diagnoses of invasive brain tumours and 4,347 diagnoses of non-invasive tumours in Britain during 2009, followed by 3,889 deaths from invasive and 995 deaths from non-invasive brain tumours in 2010.

Those figures are retained here as historical context, not as a description of the present. The original page also extrapolated a British death rate across the entire world population. That was not a sound comparison: countries have different age structures, diagnostic systems, tumour registration and health services. I have removed that estimate.

## The UK Picture in 2026

The latest figures published by Cancer Research UK report approximately **12,500 new brain, other central nervous system and intracranial tumour cases each year** in the UK, using data from 2019 and 2021–2022. That total includes malignant, non-malignant and uncertain-behaviour tumours across the brain and related structures.

Cancer Research UK reports approximately **5,600 deaths from brain tumours each year**, based on UK data from 2022–2024. It also reports that tumours affecting the brain and spinal cord are the **second most common group of cancers in children**.

These totals cannot be compared directly with every number in the old page because definitions and reporting periods differ. Brain-tumour statistics are particularly sensitive to whether non-malignant and uncertain-behaviour tumours are included. Any comparison should therefore use the same definition, population and time period.

Statistics describe populations, not individual outcomes. They combine many tumour types whose behaviour and prognosis differ enormously.

Source: [Cancer Research UK: brain, other CNS and intracranial tumour statistics](https://www.cancerresearchuk.org/health-professional/cancer-statistics/statistics-by-cancer-type/brain-other-cns-and-intracranial-tumours) and [information about brain tumours](https://www.cancerresearchuk.org/about-cancer/brain-tumours/about).

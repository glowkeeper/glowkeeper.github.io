# Missed Opportunities

In the weeks leading up to that appointment, Abi visited her doctor’s surgery on five more occasions. Sarah and I were becoming increasingly concerned because she had started vomiting. She even vomited at the practice! She was also becoming extremely lethargic and complaining that her head hurt. I remember being told that children of her age were forever picking up one illness or another and that there was probably nothing seriously wrong. So we left with another ordinary explanation: earache, a cold, perhaps the swine flu the whole country was panicking about that summer. Abi was even offered Tamiflu. Five visits. Vomiting, headaches and a child fading in front of us. Still, none of us saw the tumour.

As the weeks went by, Abi became very poorly. Her squint worsened, she became ever more lethargic, and she stopped walking. Matters weren’t helped when she came down with her sister’s chicken pox, but I was increasingly worried about her general well-being, so as soon as that illness cleared, I took her to A\&E. This was just a day or two before she was due at the eye hospital. My recollection is that her condition was attributed to the after-effects of chickenpox. I was advised to keep her hydrated, give her paracetamol if she developed a temperature and expect her to improve within a few days. She had stopped walking, and still we were sent home.

On 9 August 2009, Abi finally saw an orthoptist at the eye hospital. I remember being told that she was long-sighted. Relief came first, then confusion: how did long-sightedness explain a child who had stopped walking and could not stop vomiting? I raised both concerns but left reassured that similar symptoms were seen in other long-sighted children. As Abi needed glasses, the orthoptist referred her to an optician. That appointment, the next day, saved Abi’s life.

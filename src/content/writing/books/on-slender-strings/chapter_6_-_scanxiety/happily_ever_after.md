# Happily Ever After?

And they all lived happily ever after.

Well not quite. A recent study of children with low-grade gliomas showed that about 1 in 6 of those who had undergone a complete resection had suffered a recurrence. Even if Abi is one of the fortunate ones, she will have regular scans for at least the next decade, with all the worry they entail. She still has a shunt fitted, of course, which means that any illness brings the additional concern of whether her shunt is malfunctioning. Then there’s Abi’s squint, which will probably need an operation. So Abi’s health will still be a concern for a while yet. In fact, no matter what happens, it will probably always be a concern.

However, our future suddenly looks much brighter. We now have a six-month gap before Abi is due to have her next scan, and I intend to use that time to move on from four years of worrying about whether the ticking time bomb in Abi’s brain was about to explode. Instead of trying to find the best clinical outcome for my daughter, I can get my own life moving forward again. After all, it has necessarily been on hold while my youngest daughter has needed most of my attention.

I have created a charity to pass on what I learned about seeking second opinions and to help people find medical expertise when another opinion might make all the difference. It did for us. I also intend to complete the thesis for my postgraduate course, return to martial arts and re-establish my regular yoga and meditation practices. When under pressure, I get so wrapped up in worry that I forget to do the things that are good for me. The irony, of course, is that difficult times are when I need those things most. Never mind. Better late than never. It’s good that I’ve remembered them now.

The children will not miss out, however. Quite the contrary. I will be able to banish the worry and soul-searching over whether I am doing the right thing for Abi. Instead, my children will get a happy, active daddy. They’ll like that.

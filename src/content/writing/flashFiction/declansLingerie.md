# Declan’s Lingerie

Deep in the East End, nestled in a regenerated part of Old London Town, a peculiar structure sends shivers down the spine of those who dare to venture near it. It is a stadium that is said to be haunted by the spirit of Declan Rice, a former inhabitant who met a gruesome end under mysterious circumstances - he somehow ended up playing for Arsenal. Rumours swirled around the city about strange occurrences within the stadium’s walls. Disembodied shadows that move on their own. Whispers that seem to come from nowhere. Eerie presences that linger in the air. Many believe it is his restless spirit, seeking revenge for the injustices done to him in life. Older, darker voices whisper of a curse, sworn by a West Ham supporter to an Arsenal-supporting friend: should Arsenal ever win anything, Declan will strip down to a claret and blue bra and knickers and lift the cup with his arms crossed.

One fateful night, a group of unsuspecting Millwall supporters, West Ham’s oldest and bitterest rivals, ventured onto the Woolwich ferry (they got lost and missed the turning for the Blackwall Tunnel), determined to prove they feared nothing and to explore the home of their enemies on one of its late off-season tours. As they crept through the dark, dusty corridors, a chill ran through them, and they could feel the weight of a spirit’s gaze upon them.

Suddenly, they stumbled upon a room unlike any other they had ever seen: at the centre was a trophy cabinet, replete with the European Cup Winners’ Cup, FA Cup, Intertoto Cup, a Conference League trophy from that memorable night in Prague on 7 June 2023, and a replica of the old Jules Rimet World Cup that West Ham won in 1966. Declan Rice’s claret and blue lingerie, pressed and waiting for the fateful day, hung from the ceiling like macabre decorations. And in a corner stood a mannequin dressed in a tracksuit, its lifeless eyes staring into the void.

As the group tried to understand what they were seeing, a voice pierced the silence, chilling them to the bone. “Hello. My name’s David Moyes. Welcome to my domain,” whispered the voice darkly, though the mannequin’s lips never moved, sending a wave of terror through their souls. Realisation dawned upon them as they understood the truth - the spirits of West Ham’s Champions of Europe had not left this stadium. And they had found a way to torment the living, forever (and quite happily) trapped within the confines of a stadium that they never wanted to leave in the first place.

As the group of unsuspecting Millwall fans scrambled to escape, the walls closed in around them, the claret and blue lingerie twisting and contorting in a grotesque display of malevolence. For once, nobody liked them, and they very much cared. And through the darkness, they could hear Declan Rice yelling, “Shoot, Jarrod!” which echoed off the encroaching walls, a haunting reminder of the horror Declan’s West Ham used to inflict on opponents who dared to tread inside their domain.

Come on You Irons.

# Eric

Eric once lived in an old elm tree that stood over the Green Man Roundabout, on the outskirts of East London. That was during the days of the M11 link road protests, when intrepid dissidents, such as Eric, tried to stop East End communities, which had withstood the Blitz, from being ripped apart by the Tory Government and their road-building friends. The elm that Eric lived in had stood on that spot for 230 years. In its lifetime, it had seen the invention of the lightbulb, the railway and the internal combustion engine. All of that was much to its regret.

Eric rarely left his tree, but he had done so when he went on the Poll Tax demonstration, as he hadn't been able to resist the opportunity to venture into Central London and turn over a few cars. Later that evening, he partied at a very loud rave on the kerbside of a line of terraced houses that were being squatted to try to save them from being demolished by the road builders.

Eric returned home not having slept for 24 hours, so when he looked up at the crown of his tree, he hoped that his tired eyes were deceiving him. It was mid-summer, but many of the upper branches had browned. His eyes were telling the truth. He knew then that it was not the road that would kill this tree, but the malaise that had stalked England's elms for decades - Dutch elm disease.

Eric finally left his arboreal home when the bulldozers sealed the tree's fate a few months later. He did not put up much of a fight; the tree itself had already succumbed to the fungus ripping through its heart, and there seemed little point in defending a corpse. But by then, Eric had developed a love affair with the beautiful elms that once dominated the English countryside. So he resolved to go to one of the few places in England that had withstood the onslaught of Dutch elm disease - Brighton.

Eric moved into the hollowed-out trunk of a tree in Brighton's Royal Pavilion Gardens. It had been planted in the year that the US gained independence from the plague of the British Empire, and is thought to be one of the oldest English elms left standing in this world.

Once upon a time, we all lived in and around the trees and shared our homes with the birds, the bees, and all the other animals. But we have long since forgotten that.

The local council had forgotten too. An officer arrived with a clipboard and a laminated notice, and read out the eviction order to the man in a tree. Eric listened politely, offered him a cup of tea, and stayed put. Then came the cameras, followed by the left-leaning lobby groups, and the council discovered that evicting a bearded man from a tree as old as America made for a poor photograph. They left Eric alone.

To this day, you will find Eric living in that tree, which the council now features in its tourist brochure, Eric included. Should you pass by, be sure to buy him a cup of tea and thank him for preserving humanity's link to the natural world.

1 December, 2015

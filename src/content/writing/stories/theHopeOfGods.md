# The Hope of Gods

On the day Andrea was born, a beautiful spring morning broke, and for one fleeting moment the grown-ups who held her forgot their grudges and saw the world unsullied by the greed and self-interest of men. Even the most bitter among them felt a flash of hope, and wished that, one day, their kindred might fashion a Middle Earth worthier than the one they had inherited.

That is how it is with every child. The Gods of Asgard and the fire giants of Muspelheim both know that the children of Middle Earth are special, for within them lie all hopes and fears. Odin, the ruler of Asgard, feels each newborn’s hope as warmth, for without hope the Gods themselves would not exist. Surt, the fire giant and ruler of Muspelheim, feels it as a threat, because without fear his kind would wither and die. So the Gods and the giants have fought many a bloody war in the theatre of childhood, for the very existence of the higher and lower realms of the Nine Worlds. And because those realms have ever been, and shall ever be, the wars are eternal. That is not to say they are meaningless, for in their disorder lies a balance.

Most Middle Earthers never notice, being too busy with lesser wars of their own. The ancient battles are subtler affairs, played out in the deep psyche of the young, and the afflictions of youth are often the manifestations of the dreams and nightmares of the Gods and giants. When a child’s hope burns too brightly, Muspelheim notices, and tries to redress the balance by forcing the child to endure its malevolent intent, through illness and suffering. It is not fair. Muspelheim has never heard of fairness.

Andrea was just such a child. The Gods saw her arrive, and celebrated the new life loudly. But Muspelheim had heard too, and war had raged. Thus, Andrea had endured much during her short journey upon Middle Earth. Three times Surt had all but managed to consume her with the fears honed in his fire pits. But three times had Odin rushed to her aid, recognising that the balance of the Nine Worlds depended upon her safety. Each time he had vanquished the giant’s nightmares with a single throw of his spear Gungnir. No dream could withstand a spear that never misses its mark, thrown by a god who had once hung nine nights from Yggdrasil to learn what mattered. Dreams of the upper realms of Alfheim, the land of the Light Elves, instantly supplanted Andrea’s fire-strewn nightmares. Hope burned brightly in Andrea once more.

Odin’s final throw was the mightiest of them all, and for a while it seemed as though the war had been won and Andrea’s nightmares had been banished forever. But the giants of Muspelheim harbour a ferocious bitterness toward the Gods, for without them they could extend the dark realms unhindered. So they do not give up easily. Barely a year passed before Andrea’s dreams turned dark once more. But whereas previously the war had raged in her head, this time it was fought deep in her belly, where the fears of the young are said to nest. As the Gods and giants began skirmishes, Andrea began experiencing more and more nightmares, which the forces of Muspelheim were fashioning in an attempt to draw her into the underworld. These began to take a terrible toll on Andrea’s health, because she was forced to vomit violently in order to expunge the traces of poison that the dreams left behind. This put her in great danger, for she was unable to hold down the food that all Middle Earthers depend upon for sustenance.

The Gods were not unaware of the mortal needs of man, and they realised that Andrea was becoming quite poorly. So they arranged for Andrea to be rushed into hospital. These were dangerous times for all public services, because the Government of the time had been heavily infiltrated by giants. They had instigated a mean-spirited programme of austerity measures on the weak and the poor, and were busy selling off key resources to their kin in Svartalfheim, in order to feed their greed, so that they could hammer the needy ever harder and spread fear ever wider. The hospitals were particularly vulnerable, because the dark forces recognised them as a place for good and were desperately trying to bring about their ruin through ‘efficiencies’, which were really a ruse for stripping the hospitals so bare they could barely function. Nevertheless, they remained a safe haven for the very needy, for the hospital front lines were still a stronghold of the Light Elves, who were forces for good, making a stand and fighting the ancient battles against their foes from Muspelheim.

Thankfully for Andrea, she was delivered to one such battle-hardened hospital unit. Odin ensured that it was so. The Senior Consultant on her ward was Freyr himself, ruler of Alfheim, walking among mortals in a white coat, as the Gods sometimes do. He submerged Andrea in love to help her overcome the fire-laden dreams that the giants had forced her to endure. When Freyr couldn’t fight alongside Andrea, he would send his twin sister Freya, the Goddess of Love, to care for her instead.

The giants began to turn up the pressure. Grýla, the ogress who steals children at Yuletide, forced Andrea to dream of being tied up in a sack and dragged down to the pits of Hel. Freya countered by gently circling her right index finger on Andrea’s wrist, which represented the sack being untied so that Andrea could escape.

Then a troll appeared in a dream as her best friend, Elfie. Elfie and Andrea laughed and giggled as they played a happy game of Stuck-in-the-mud in the school playground. But then the troll suddenly transformed into its true form, with sickly green scaly skin and mocking eyes. It cast Andrea into a shadowy cave with flickering flame, where frightening silhouettes flitted across the dank walls. Freya nullified this act by cupping Andrea’s hands ever tighter into hers so she could feel the warmth of her soft skin. They walked hand in hand away from the horror of the cave.

The trickster Loki was the next to try to lure Andrea down into the depths of Muspelheim. He did so by pretending to be her father. Andrea sat on his knee and played lovingly, becoming enthralled by a pattern that the folds of her skirt had fashioned. When she turned back to her father, to tell him that the folds looked like a dragon, he had transformed into a great grey wolf with fiery red eyes and large, snarling teeth. He snapped at her neck. She flinched, avoiding the blow, and let out a scream that transcended her dream and pierced the real world. Freya knew it at once and wrapped her arms around Andrea, gently kissing her on her forehead. The kiss broke through Andrea’s nightmare as the loving embrace of her mother, and with that act, Loki’s subterfuge was defeated.

Andrea was becoming weaker because the nightmares were leaving even greater traces of poison. Andrea needed more help, so every morning, when she returned from the cold, stark corners of her nightmares, the Gods began rewarding her with gifts. These were charms imbued with a great deal of shield magic. They were fashioned to help her escape from the clutches of the dark forces and make her nighttime dreams much more pleasant, thus preventing her from being sick. They were also intended to be trinkets for her to look forward to, thus ensuring that she wanted to return from her dreams every morning, no matter how hard her ordeals. Even the bear she hugged was really a berserker, a divine warrior in a bear’s skin, capable of countering the battle strategies of the giants.

However, the Gods knew that, despite their help and all the charms around Andrea’s bedside, nothing they did could force her to find hope; she had to kindle that herself. So Freyr decided to give Andrea something to reach for. It would not be a test, for the love of the Gods is not a prize to be won. Odin knows that all children are held in the loving embrace of Asgard, for it is the children themselves who sustain it, through hope and belief. Those burdened with the touch of Muspelheim, who still show great courage, receive the most help of all, for it is they who sow the seeds of unending hope amongst all children. So Andrea would not be abandoned, no matter what the outcome. But hope grows strongest when it has somewhere to go, and a child who has found her own courage once will find it again. Nevertheless, much was at stake.

Freyr borrowed his sister’s golden necklace, Brisingamen, known throughout the Nine Worlds, and hung it from a branch of the great tree Laerad, which stood at the far side of the forest at the Northern boundary of Valhalla. As Andrea slept peacefully, he then envisioned a dream for her that placed her in the land of the fallen heroes, many valleys and mountains away from the necklace, and whispered gently in her ear, “Go find that necklace, sweet angel.” Both Freyr and his twin sister Freya then sat by her bedside and worried about the little girl who had already been through so much. Andrea had been granted the gift of great resolve, so in that regard the Gods felt they needn’t worry, for they could have asked for no greater ally. So Freyr and Freya cast aside what concerns they could, clasped hands and tried to conjure a golden light around each other’s hearts, in order to foster hope. Then they waited.

Andrea first dreamt of a cave underneath a vast mountain range. But where the giants’ caves had been dank and forbidding, the caverns here were warm and welcoming, lit brightly by great torches. She giggled as she imagined that the torchlight was in fact a frog jumping across the walls. This attracted the attention of the dwarf Alviss the Wise, who had not heard laughter in those parts for longer than it took to fashion a golden tiara fit for a queen (which took a very long time). He was delighted by Andrea’s cheeky demeanour, and resolved to guide her safe passage through to the other side of the range. This took many hours, during which the pair became the most unlikely of friends. So much so that, when Alviss had taken Andrea through the range and out to the Great Plains, he gave her the gift of his greatest wisdom: “Follow your heart, and not your mind, and you will not fail to find the right path. For the heart is steadfast, but the mind plays tricks.” Andrea thanked Alviss for his help and planted a tender kiss on her dear friend’s cheek, which made the old dwarf blush like a teenager. He then bade her farewell, and smiled the smile of happy memories of their time together as he watched her head out across the valley alone. But this did not concern him, for he knew that such a gentle angel would not have far to go before receiving more help.

He was right, because help soon appeared in the form of the golden horse Gulltop, who was busy grazing on the vast green grasslands. This was the God Heimdall’s horse, sent to aid Andrea on her quest, for Heimdall knew of the great battles she had fought and understood she needed more help because much was at stake. Gulltop trotted over towards Andrea, neighed joyfully, and nuzzled her neck lovingly. Andrea was delighted. She clasped her arms around Gulltop’s great neck in order to plant a kiss on his strong jowl, and she gasped as she was hoisted onto the horse’s broad back. But intuition told her to trust, just as Alviss had advised, so she brought her face up to the mighty stallion’s right ear, and whispered, “Take me wherever your heart so wishes, beautiful horse.” Gulltop obliged and galloped like the wind, crossing the plains in the time it takes Heimdall to hear the grass grow. He slowed as a line of trees spread out before them and then he allowed Andrea to climb down from his flanks. He began to head back to the lush green grasses he had been happily grazing upon, but not before letting the lovely child tenderly stroke his mane in gratitude for her safe passage. Andrea watched as a flash of gold disappeared over the horizon, before she began to push her way onwards and make her way through the woods.

This was no dark, unwelcoming affair of the giants’ making, dead to all but the shadows, but an ancient oak woodland, bristling with the chatter of birds and full of age-old folklore and the magic of the healers. Andrea felt loved and became enthralled by a happy reverie of the great oaks that lay before her.

Then the light began to fail. A red glow crept between the trunks, the birdsong stopped, and the air grew thick with the smell of smoke. A wall of fire rose up across the path, and in it she glimpsed a shape she knew from her worst nights, vast and crowned with flame: Surt himself, who had followed her even here. Andrea’s heart hammered. Her mind told her what any mind would: turn back, hide, run. But she remembered what Alviss had said about the mind and its tricks. She remembered the sack, the cave and the wolf, and how every one of them had come undone by a hand held tight. So she pressed her own hands together, the way Freya had held them, and walked straight into the flames.

They gave off no heat at all. Surt’s fire had only ever been made of fear, and it could not burn a heart that was steady. The flames guttered, the great shape howled, and then it was gone, and Andrea was standing in clear green light, shaking, but quite unburned.

Then the great boar Gullinbursti, who had been waiting for her on the far side, darted out in front of her. Andrea imagined that Gullinbursti looked over his left shoulder and beckoned her to follow. Again she resolved to follow Alviss’s advice and listen to her intuition that she should run after the boar. She lost the trail a number of times, but each time she would hear the great boar’s grunt and spy a quick flash of his vast hindquarters. Before she knew it, she was at the far edge of the ancient forest, at the Northernmost reaches of Valhalla. Somehow she understood that the boar had disappeared from her gaze forever, but before she could bemoan her loss, she spied the noble stag Eikthyrnir feeding on the green leaves of a magnificent oak. This was Laerad.

Andrea wandered over quietly, hoping to get closer to the stag, for he was a beautiful creature who exuded love and Andrea felt the need to touch him. Of course, the stag knew of Andrea’s coming, so he could have just as well stomped over loudly instead. But just as Andrea got within touching distance, Eikthyrnir reared on his hind legs in order to reach up to a branch with particularly lush green leaves. Andrea watched in awe, but just then, she spied Brisingamen and gasped. She reached up, took the necklace, and clasped it around her slender neck. With this act, she reached the end of Freyr’s quest. And as she did so, she awoke.

Andrea’s father was somewhat startled to see his daughter wake up and sit upright so suddenly. But he was also pleased to see her looking as well as she had in a good while. “Good morning, my angel,” he said breezily.

“I’m hungry, Daddy!” replied Andrea, somewhat surprised to feel so.

“The nurse thought you might be! I don’t know how, but it seems she was right. She left you these honey-drizzled crumpets.”

Andrea gobbled them up greedily. Andrea’s father had not seen her eat like that in months. He was so excited he somehow missed the beautiful necklace that had magically appeared around Andrea’s neck.

The Gods rejoiced. They had thwarted the giants of Muspelheim once more. The Gods, Light Elves and their allies fighting on the front lines of the hospital wards were victorious. What’s more, they were confident that if Muspelheim had further ordeals planned for Andrea, she would fight the good battle alongside them. By doing so, the eternal hope that kept the Gods secure in Asgard would be held safe in Andrea’s heart. The future looked bright.
